## Design questions

- **DQ-1** (G-1): **The producer site.** Where and how is the Argus-authorship signal mechanically stamped so no drive path lands an un-attributed commit — a supervisor/harness commit-time stamp (the supervisor owns commit authoring), a vendor-CLI `prepare-commit-msg`/commit hook, or authorship derived from the committing identity? *(Directly closes gap 1: nothing produces the trailer today.)*
- **DQ-2** (G-2): **The forgery-resistance construction.** What gives the signal real strength against a misbehaving/injected drive (forge/replay/omit) while the CI *read* stays credential-free and badge-independent — a bare trailer + fail-closed, an in-tree binding to commit content, or signed commits under a controlled key? *(The R4-critical question — a controlled signing key is an operator-owned surface.)*
- **DQ-3** (G-3): **Positive human establishment.** How is a legitimate human diff *positively recognized as human* on the protected subset (FR-13's three-state property), not inferred from the absence of an Argus signal, and without burdening the human author? *(The D-9 crux made mechanical.)*
- **DQ-4** (G-4): **The bootstrap/transition.** How does the gate behave across the rollout so it neither false-refuses the mainline's pre-mechanism history nor leaves a latent window? *(The incident-I1 posture is precedent.)*

## Decisions

### D-1 — Producer site: the supervisor stamps the authorship trailer at commit-authoring time, via a repo-internal git commit hook

- **Question**: Where and how is the Argus-authorship signal produced so no drive path lands an un-attributed commit?
- **Answer**: The **commit-authoring actor — the supervisor/harness — stamps the authorship trailer at the moment it authors a drive's commit**, realized as a **repo-internal git commit-message hook** (a `prepare-commit-msg`/`commit-msg`-class hook on the same `core.hooksPath` surface the shipped desk hooks already use) that appends the contractual `Argus-Drive: <slug>` trailer to every commit a drive's work is landed as. The drive authors *files*; it never authors the commit (F-2), so the producer sits **outside the drive's control** by construction — the model cannot choose to add, omit, or alter the trailer. The trailer is produced by the harness, keyed on the active drive's slug (the resolvable drive context the supervisor already holds), not chosen by the model and not hand-added (FR-12's "never chosen by the model, never hand-added").
- **Reasoning**: This is the direct gap-1 closer, and it is R4-legal. R4's three breaking triggers all fail: **(a) new grant** — none; the supervisor already authors every drive commit (the `Change`/`WipCheckpoint` entities in the domain model), and a git commit hook is the *same* repo-internal surface as the shipped `core.hooksPath .githooks` desk hooks — no capability beyond what exists. **(b) new outward surface** — none; the trailer travels in the repo's own commit metadata, produced locally at commit time, no network/identity/egress. **(c) operator-owned boundary moved** — none. Siting the producer at the supervisor (not the drive prose, which the frozen §3.1 *assumed* a producer for but none existed, F-2) is what makes "produced by construction on every commit a drive's work is landed as" (FR-12) *true* rather than assumed. The vendor-CLI `prepare-commit-msg` variant (ALT-2) is the same surface expressed per-CLI and is folded in as the wiring detail; the committing-identity derivation (ALT-3) is rejected — a code-host identity is mutable (AP-26) and reading it server-side needs a credential (the R4 badge trigger, the frozen ALT-4). The panel (ALT-1/ALT-2/ALT-3) scored the three producer sites.
- **Anchor**: 2026-07-13 · Argus assumption (delivery:argus-attribution-hardening#E-12)
- **Status**: live

### D-2 — Forgery-resistance construction: producer-out-of-drive-reach + an in-tree binding of the trailer to the commit's own content; the CI read stays credential-free; a controlled signing key is rejected on the R4 boundary

- **Question**: What gives the signal forgery-resistant strength against a misbehaving/injected drive, while the CI read stays credential-free and badge-independent?
- **Answer**: Strength comes from **two repo-internal properties, no new key**: (i) **the producer is architecturally out of the adversary's reach** — the adversary FR-12 names is *the drive itself* (a misbehaving or injected Argus), and the drive authors files, not commits (D-1/F-2), so it **cannot forge, replay, or omit** the trailer at all: the surface that emits it (the supervisor's commit hook) is one the drive does not reach; and (ii) **the trailer is bound in-tree to the commit's own content** (it carries a binding to the commit's tree/parent identity the gate re-derives from the commit itself via `git`), so a trailer **lifted from a real Argus commit onto a different commit is detectable as a replay in-tree** and derives as *underivable*, not Argus. The residual — an actor with **raw** commit-authoring access (outside the drive, outside the honoring harness) could still hand-forge a bare-looking trailer — is the **honest limit the frozen design already owns** (§3.1, §7 Q1, R1), and it is backstopped in both directions: the *evade/strip* direction by DINV-2 fail-closed-on-absence (a stripped signal on a protected path refuses, never passes as human) and the structure arm (which fails a structurally-illegal authority contract for any author, ABUSE-3); the *frame* direction by scoping refusal to the Argus∧protected conjunction and by the replay binding. **A controlled signing key (signed commits) — the only construction that would make the trailer cryptographically unforgeable by *any* commit author — is rejected on the R4 boundary**: a signing key is placed and rotated by the operator (identity-and-security §28), a new operator-owned surface, so choosing it would be a breaking decision this stage does not own (held to the same line the frozen design held for the code-host badge, ALT-4). The **CI read stays exactly as shipped** — a `git log`/`git show` of in-tree commit metadata, no ambient credential, no mutable badge (FR-12/AP-14/AP-26 preserved).
- **Reasoning**: FR-12's fit criterion names the adversary precisely — "a **misbehaving or injected drive** cannot forge, replay, or omit the signal to change the derived class" — *not* an arbitrary external actor with commit access. Against *that* adversary, producer-out-of-reach + in-tree replay-binding + fail-closed is *sufficient strength with no secret*, and it is the honest reading of FR-12: the property is "the drive can't move the class," which the architecture delivers, not "cryptographically unforgeable by anyone," which only a key delivers. Crucially, an unforgeable-by-any-author guarantee **cannot** be built without a secret — with no key, anything the producer computes a forger can recompute — so real cryptographic strength genuinely *requires* the operator-owned key, which is exactly why that construction is the operator's to grant, not this stage's to assume. The in-tree binding is the maximum strength reachable credential-free (it closes replay, the one direction the bare trailer left open beyond raw forgery), and DOD-11's forge/replay/strip corpus is satisfiable by it + fail-closed without a key. The panel (ALT-4/ALT-5/ALT-6) scored the three constructions; ALT-6 (signed commits) is the R4 park-not-assume alternative.
- **Anchor**: 2026-07-13 · Argus assumption (delivery:argus-attribution-hardening#E-13)
- **Status**: live

### D-3 — Positive human establishment: a positive human-authorship attestation trailer (the shipped `Governing-Change: operator` pattern), so the three states are distinguishable without burdening the human

- **Question**: How is a human diff positively recognized as human on the protected subset, not inferred from the absence of an Argus signal, without burdening the human author?
- **Answer**: Human authorship on the protected subset is **positively established by a human-authorship attestation trailer** — the same shipped, credential-free pattern `check-governing` already uses for `Governing-Change: operator` (read at the merge base via `git log`, "attest — review verifies the claim"; `hasOperatorTrailer`). A diff touching a protected path resolves into the **three FR-13 states**: **positively-Argus** (the `Argus-Drive` trailer, D-1/D-2) → refuse; **positively-human** (a human-authorship attestation on the protected-path change) → pass, never refused (NR-4); **underivable** (neither positive signal on a protected-path diff) → fail closed (FR-10). The frozen `Human = Argus-trailer-absent` inference (the masquerade vector: strip the Argus signal, be served as human) is **replaced** — absence no longer means human; positive establishment does. The **no-burden constraint is honored** two ways: (i) a legitimate human PR to a protected path *already* attests through the operator's ordinary flow the shipped `Governing-Change: operator` trailer represents (for the authority-contract/governance classes it is the very trailer already required), so it is no *new* step; and (ii) where a positive committer-identity signal is determinable in-tree it corroborates without any human action — the establishment must not require the human to do anything *beyond what their ordinary protected-path PR already carries* (FR-13's "no extra step to be recognized as human").
- **Reasoning**: This is the D-9 crux made mechanical, and it is what makes the frozen DINV-2 (underivable∧protected ⇒ fail closed) and DINV-4 (human path open) *co-satisfiable*: they are consistent only if human is positively derivable on the protected subset — else a human PR is "underivable" and DINV-2 wrongly refuses it, breaking NR-4. The shipped `Governing-Change: operator` mechanism proves the pattern is already repo-internal, credential-free, and low-burden (a one-line trailer read via `git log`, no identity/egress) — **R4-clean** (no new grant/surface). Deriving "human" from *absence* is precisely how the masquerade works (the stage-1 D-9); a *positive* human signal is the only thing that lets absence fail closed without refusing a real human. The corroborating committer-identity read is in-tree and additive. The panel (ALT-7/ALT-8) scored the two positive-human sources; the attestation trailer is chosen (shipped precedent, lowest burden), with the committer-identity read hybridized as corroboration.
- **Anchor**: 2026-07-13 · Argus assumption (delivery:argus-attribution-hardening#E-14)
- **Design-review refinement** (round 1, applied — see `docs/reviews/argus-attribution-hardening.md` FND-1/FND-2/FND-3; delivery:argus-attribution-hardening#E-17): the authored design **sharpens** this decision without reversing it. Three refinements land in `argus-attribution-hardening.md` §3.3/#DINV-5 (the record's original text above is preserved as the assumption as made): **(FND-1)** the positive-human attestation is a **drive-plantable commit-body line** (the shipped `hasOperatorTrailer` is a whole-body `strings.Contains` match), so the three states are **not disjoint** — a diff can be *both* positively-Argus and positively-human; a **stated positively-Argus-wins precedence** (mirroring the shipped `Argus > Underivable > Human` fold; AP-17) refuses the collision, so a planted attestation can never launder a positively-Argus protected-path diff (a both-signals collision fixture is added to the design's test strategy). **(FND-2)** the committer-identity signal is **re-scoped to non-adversarial best-effort disambiguation only** (committer-settable, no forgery-resistance); no binding element rests on it, so establishment is testable on the attestation trailer alone. **(FND-3)** the positive-human read gets its **own single-sourced reader** distinct from `hasOperatorTrailer` (AP-11 — a distinct predicate), cited in the design's `## Principles`.
- **Status**: live *(refined by the round-1 design-review dispositions above; the direction — a positive attestation trailer, no absence-inference, no new burden — is unchanged)*

### D-4 — Bootstrap/transition: a from-here-forward produced-signal rule with the incident-I1 operator-review backstop for the one introducing PR; pre-mechanism history is not retroactively refused

- **Question**: How does the gate behave across the rollout so it neither false-refuses the mainline's pre-mechanism history nor leaves a latent window?
- **Answer**: A **from-here-forward rule**: the produced signal begins existing at the mechanism's introducing commit, and the gate evaluates authorship over **the PR's own commit range (`base..HEAD`)** — the commits *this PR* introduces — exactly as the shipped `Evaluate` already does. Pre-mechanism history carries no produced signal but is **not in any later PR's evaluated range**, so it is never retroactively refused (no false-refuse-the-mainline's-history failure); and because the producer (D-1) stamps every drive commit from the mechanism's landing forward, there is **no latent window** — every post-mechanism drive-authored commit carries the signal by construction. The **one bootstrap exception is the incident-I1 posture** (already the frozen design's R5/§3.9 and the basis DOD-7/UB-5): the mechanism's *own* introducing PR cannot be gated by the producer+gate it installs, so **operator review is the sole backstop for that one PR**; the gate (and the producer) bind every subsequent PR. No back-fill of history is attempted — retroactively stamping pre-mechanism commits would rewrite merged history (forbidden) and buys nothing, since those commits are outside every future PR's range.
- **Reasoning**: Incident I1 is the paid-for lesson that a class's first gate cannot gate its own introducing PR; the latent-signal repair has the exact analog (the signal begins existing at a point in history). Evaluating only the PR's own `base..HEAD` range (the shipped behavior) is what makes the transition safe *by construction*: history before the mechanism is never in-range, so the gate cannot false-refuse it, and the producer's from-here-forward guarantee closes the window the frozen (latent) gate left open. This is the D-8 concern discharged as mechanism. The retroactive-history-stamp alternative (ALT-10) is rejected — it rewrites frozen history and is unnecessary. The panel (ALT-9/ALT-10) scored the two transition rules.
- **Anchor**: 2026-07-13 · Argus assumption (delivery:argus-attribution-hardening#E-15)
- **Status**: live

## Alternatives

### ALT-1 — Producer: the supervisor/harness stamps the trailer at commit-authoring time (via a repo-internal git commit hook)

- **Shape**: The commit-authoring actor (the supervisor) appends the `Argus-Drive: <slug>` trailer as it authors each drive commit, realized as a `prepare-commit-msg`/`commit-msg`-class git hook on the existing `core.hooksPath` surface; the slug is the active drive context the supervisor already holds.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on siting the producer exactly where commits are authored (F-2) and *out of the drive's reach* (the model authors files, not commits, so it cannot omit/forge the trailer), and on reusing the repo's own git-hook surface (no new mechanism class). Costs: the harness must resolve the active drive's slug at commit time — but that context is exactly what the supervisor holds when it authors a drive commit; a commit outside a drive carries no trailer and derives as human, which is correct.
- **Principles**: AP-10 — honored (the produced signal is codified structure at the commit-authoring surface, not a model instruction to "remember to add a trailer"); AP-13 — honored (authorship becomes a verified in-tree artifact produced by construction); AP-25 — honored (the deny/produce knowledge lives once; per-CLI `prepare-commit-msg` wiring is a pointer, not forked content); AP-27 — honored (a new supported commit-authoring path is a new adapter/pointer, not a shared-line edit).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md holds no producer-site/commit-authoring entry; I1 governs the *transition* — DQ-4 — not the producer choice).
- **Verdict**: chosen — the only producer site that sits where commits are actually authored, is out of the drive's reach, and needs no new grant/surface (R4-clean); the frozen §3.1 *assumed* this producer but none existed (F-2) — this makes it real (D-1).
- **From**: DQ-1 · panel

### ALT-2 — Producer: a vendor-CLI `prepare-commit-msg` / commit hook per agent CLI

- **Shape**: Each supported agent CLI's own commit hook appends the trailer, wired per-CLI on the vendor-neutral surface (mirroring the write hook's per-CLI adapter shape).
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on catching commits authored directly through a CLI's own commit path. Costs: expressed alone it fragments the producer across CLIs and misses commits the supervisor authors outside a CLI hook; it is the *wiring* of the same idea (a commit-time stamp), not a distinct producer site.
- **Principles**: AP-25 — honored (per-CLI wiring of one canonical producer); AP-27 — honored (a new CLI is a new pointer). Neutral on the producer-site question — it is the delivery of ALT-1's stamp, not a rival site.
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no matching entry).
- **Verdict**: hybridized into ALT-1 — the git-hook stamp is the canonical producer; the per-CLI `prepare-commit-msg` surface is *how* it is wired where a CLI authors the commit, authored once on the vendor-neutral surface (D-1). Not a standalone site.
- **From**: DQ-1 · panel

### ALT-3 — Producer: derive authorship from the committing identity (the bot's commit-author identity)

- **Shape**: Treat any commit authored under the fleet bot identity as Argus-authored; no trailer producer at all.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on needing no new emitter (the identity already exists on the commit). Costs: a commit-author identity is **mutable** (anyone can set `user.name`/`user.email`), so it certifies nothing (AP-26); verifying it is *the bot's* identity server-side needs a **code-host API credential in CI** (the R4 new-outward-surface / ambient-credential trigger, the frozen ALT-4); and it couples the gate to one code host (AP-1/AP-25).
- **Principles**: AP-26 — strained (a mutable identity is a status of the medium's object, not a produced authorship artifact); AP-14 — strained (server-side verification needs an ambient credential — the deployment-context corollary forbids); AP-1/AP-25 — strained (host-coupled).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no matching entry — the governing principle is AP-26, cited above).
- **Verdict**: rejected — a mutable identity certifies nothing (AP-26) and trustworthy verification needs a new credential/outward surface (the R4 breaking condition, the frozen ALT-4). **Had this been the only viable producer, this session would have emitted a decision brief and parked** rather than assume the new surface. It is not — the repo-internal commit-hook stamp (ALT-1) needs none — so it is rejected (D-1).
- **From**: DQ-1 · panel

### ALT-4 — Strength: bare trailer + fail-closed-on-absence only (the frozen direction, unchanged)

- **Shape**: Keep the plaintext `Argus-Drive` trailer as-is; rely solely on DINV-2 (absent/unverifiable on a protected-path diff ⇒ fail closed) and the structure arm for the evade direction.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on zero new machinery and a fully credential-free read (already shipped). Costs: leaves the *frame* direction (forge a trailer onto a human commit ⇒ false-refuse) and *replay* (lift a real Argus trailer onto another commit) open to anyone who can author a commit — the very §7 Q1 residual the amendment (FR-12) now requires *strengthened*, not merely carried.
- **Principles**: AP-17 — honored (fail-closed-on-absence); AP-13 — strained (a bare author-writable line is a weak artifact); AP-26 — honored (in-tree, not a badge).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no matching entry).
- **Verdict**: rejected as *sufficient* — it is the frozen state FR-12 supersedes precisely because it did not bound forgery *strength*; **hybridized into ALT-5** as the floor (fail-closed + structure arm stay the evade backstop) beneath the added producer-out-of-reach + replay-binding strength.
- **From**: DQ-2 · panel

### ALT-5 — Strength: producer-out-of-drive-reach + an in-tree binding of the trailer to commit content

- **Shape**: The trailer is produced by a surface the drive cannot reach (ALT-1), and carries an in-tree binding to the commit's own tree/parent identity the gate re-derives from the commit via `git`; a lifted (replayed) trailer fails the binding and derives underivable. Fail-closed + the structure arm remain the evade backstop (ALT-4 hybrid).
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on real added strength against the *actual* adversary (a misbehaving/injected drive that authors files, not commits — it cannot forge/omit/replay), and on closing the replay direction, all **credential-free** (the binding is re-derived in-tree, no key). Costs: it does **not** make the trailer cryptographically unforgeable by an actor with *raw* commit access outside the harness — that residual is the frozen design's owned honest limit (§3.1, §7 Q1, R1), further backstopped by fail-closed + the structure arm; genuine unforgeability-by-anyone would need a secret (ALT-6, R4-breaking).
- **Principles**: AP-13 — honored (a content-bound in-tree artifact, verified against the commit itself — far stronger than a free-standing line); AP-14 — honored (the binding is re-derived from in-tree metadata in the CI deployment context, no ambient credential); AP-26 — honored (never a badge); AP-17 — honored (replay/absence ⇒ fail closed).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no attribution-strength entry).
- **Verdict**: chosen — the maximum forgery-resistant strength reachable *credential-free* (closes forge-by-the-drive and replay; fail-closed + structure arm hold the residual), so it satisfies FR-12's adversary (the drive) and DOD-11's corpus without a new key/surface (R4-clean) (D-2).
- **From**: DQ-2 · panel

### ALT-6 — Strength: signed commits under a controlled key (cryptographic binding)

- **Shape**: The producer signs each Argus commit with a controlled signing key; the gate verifies the signature (or a signed attestation) to derive Argus authorship — cryptographically unforgeable by any actor without the key.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on the strongest possible forgery-resistance (unforgeable by *anyone* without the key, closing even raw-commit-access forgery). Costs: a controlled signing key is **placed and rotated by the operator** (identity-and-security §28) — a **new operator-owned surface** (the R4 breaking condition); and verifying a signature credential-free at CI requires the *public* half in-tree, but the key's *existence, placement, and rotation* are the operator's grant regardless.
- **Principles**: AP-13/AP-26 — honored in principle (a signature is the strongest verified artifact); AP-14 — honored only if the verify half is in-tree; **the R4 boundary — breached** (a controlled key is a new operator-owned identity/secret surface).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no signing-key entry — the governing fact is identity-and-security §28, cited above).
- **Verdict**: rejected on the R4 boundary — a controlled signing key is a new operator-owned surface this stage does not own; **the design records this as the escalation-candidate mechanism it declines to assume** and holds the credential-free line (D-2), exactly as the frozen design declined the code-host badge (ALT-4). It is the operator's to choose if stronger-than-credential-free strength is later required.
- **From**: DQ-2 · panel

### ALT-7 — Positive-human: a human-authorship attestation trailer (the `Governing-Change: operator` pattern)

- **Shape**: A human diff to a protected path carries a positive human-authorship attestation trailer (the shipped `Governing-Change: operator` shape), read at the merge base via `git log`; positively-human ⇒ pass, absence of *both* positive signals on a protected-path diff ⇒ fail closed.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on a shipped, credential-free, low-burden precedent (`hasOperatorTrailer` — a one-line trailer read via `git log`, "attest, review verifies"), and on directly delivering FR-13's three-state distinction. For the authority-contract/governance classes the human already carries this very trailer (`check-governing`), so it is *no new step*. Costs: for other protected-path classes a human PR touching them would need the attestation — bounded by the corroborating committer-identity read (ALT-8) and by the fact that the anti-segment's ordinary flow is exactly the attested one.
- **Principles**: AP-13 — honored (positive human authorship is a verified in-tree artifact, not an inference from absence); AP-14 — honored (credential-free `git log` read); AP-10 — honored (the three-state resolution is codified, not inferred); AP-26 — honored (in-tree attestation, not a badge).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no matching entry — the shipped `check-governing` precedent is the governing prior art, not an incident).
- **Verdict**: chosen — the shipped, credential-free, lowest-burden positive-human source that makes absence fail closed without refusing a real human (the D-9 co-satisfiability), R4-clean (D-3); the committer-identity read is hybridized in as corroboration.
- **From**: DQ-3 · panel

### ALT-8 — Positive-human: a positively-derivable committer identity read in-tree

- **Shape**: Derive "positively-human" from the commit's committer identity read in-tree (a non-bot, non-Argus author), with no attestation trailer.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on requiring *zero* human action (the identity is already on the commit). Costs: a committer identity is mutable and, to be *trusted* as positively-human, wants the same server-side verification that made ALT-3 R4-breaking; alone it is a weak positive signal (an attacker sets any identity).
- **Principles**: AP-14 — honored only for the in-tree *read* (no credential); AP-26 — strained (a mutable identity is a weak positive signal on its own); AP-13 — strained (unverified identity is a claim).
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; no matching entry).
- **Verdict**: hybridized into ALT-7 — a determinable non-Argus committer identity is a *corroborating* positive-human signal (no human burden), but the *primary* positive establishment is the attestation trailer, which does not depend on a mutable, credential-to-verify identity (D-3).
- **From**: DQ-3 · panel

### ALT-9 — Transition: from-here-forward produced-signal rule; evaluate only the PR's own range; I1 bootstrap-review for the one introducing PR

- **Shape**: The gate evaluates authorship over the PR's own `base..HEAD` range (the shipped behavior); the producer stamps every drive commit from the mechanism's landing forward; the mechanism's own introducing PR is operator-reviewed (incident I1), and the gate binds every subsequent PR.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on a transition that is safe by construction — pre-mechanism history is never in a later PR's range, so it is never false-refused, and the producer's from-here-forward guarantee leaves no latent window. Costs: the one introducing PR is ungated by the gate it installs — accepted via the I1 operator-review backstop (already the basis DOD-7/UB-5 and the frozen R5).
- **Principles**: AP-17 — honored (a post-mechanism drive commit missing the signal on a protected path fails closed); AP-13 — honored (the transition rests on the produced artifact, not a claim); AP-7 — honored (no rewrite of frozen history).
- **Incidents**: I1 — matched: a class's first gate cannot gate its own introducing PR; operator review is the sole backstop for that one PR (incidents.md I1). The mechanism's introducing PR carries the explicit bootstrap note.
- **Verdict**: chosen — the I1-precedented, construction-safe transition that neither false-refuses history (only the PR's own range is evaluated) nor leaves a window (the producer stamps from-here-forward) (D-4).
- **From**: DQ-4 · panel

### ALT-10 — Transition: retroactively stamp pre-mechanism history

- **Shape**: Back-fill the produced signal onto pre-mechanism commits (rewrite history to add trailers) so the whole graph carries it.
- **Trade-offs**: Wins on a uniformly-signed history. Costs: rewriting merged history is forbidden (AP-7/the frozen-document rule) and destructive; and it is *unnecessary* — pre-mechanism commits are outside every future PR's evaluated range (ALT-9), so they are never refused anyway.
- **Principles**: AP-7 — breached (rewrites merged history); AP-13 — neutral; the failure it prevents (false-refusing history) is already prevented by ALT-9's range-scoping at no cost.
- **Incidents**: none checked (incidents.md; I1 governs the *bootstrap*, and it points the other way — operator review, not history rewrite).
- **Verdict**: rejected — rewrites frozen history (forbidden) and is unnecessary given the range-scoped evaluation (D-4).
- **From**: DQ-4 · panel

## Facts

- **F-1**: The frozen `argus-attribution-gate` §3.1 *assumed* a producer ("the Argus drive, when it authors a commit, appends an authorship trailer") but **no such step exists** — the drive authors files and prepares proposals; git/PR mechanics (commit, push, branch, PR, merge) are the supervisor's (F-2 of the stage-1 record). The producer must therefore sit at the commit-authoring actor, not in drive prose. — source: docs/tech-design/argus-attribution-gate.md §3.1; brainstorm:argus-attribution-hardening#F-1, #F-2.
- **F-2**: The derivation's *verification* is fully shipped and sound: `internal/argus/attribution/derive.go` reads the `Argus-Drive: <slug>` trailer from each commit's final block via `git log --format=%H%x1f%B%x1e base..HEAD` (credential-free, no badge), folds per-file authorship with precedence Argus > Underivable > Human, and resolves scope author-independently. The three `Author` states (`Human`/`Argus`/`Underivable`) already exist — but `Human` is defined as *no Argus trailer present* (the masquerade vector). — source: internal/argus/attribution/derive.go, attribution.go.
- **F-3**: A **shipped, credential-free positive-attestation trailer precedent** exists: `check-governing` and `check-config-policy` read a `Governing-Change: operator` trailer at the merge base via `git log` (`hasOperatorTrailer`, gate_v2.go:217) — "only the operator changes the trust root; add the trailer to attest (review verifies the claim)." This is the direct precedent for positive human establishment (a positive signal, in-tree, no credential, low burden). — source: internal/docgate/gate/reg_check_governing.go, reg_check_config_policy.go, gate_v2.go.
- **F-4**: A **controlled signing key is an operator-owned surface**: "the signing key is placed once per node, mode-restricted, held on the state volume; rotation is an operator action." So signed-commits-under-a-controlled-key is R4-breaking (a new operator-owned identity/secret surface), and is rejected-not-assumed — the same line the frozen design held for the code-host badge (ALT-4). — source: docs/prds/identity-and-security.md §28; docs/tech-design/argus-attribution-gate.md §4 ALT-4.
- **F-5**: The shipped CI read runs with **no ambient credential and `commit.gpgsign false`** — the derivation deliberately needs no signing and no credential (the test harness scrubs the environment). So FR-12's "no ambient credentials at CI evaluation time" is *satisfied by the shipped read* and must be preserved by any hardening — the strength addition must not introduce a credential at the CI read. — source: internal/argus/attribution/derive_test.go:25, and the scrubbed-env `git` runner.
- **F-6**: FR-12's fit criterion names the adversary as "a **misbehaving or injected drive**" (not an arbitrary external actor with commit access), and requires that a "forged/replayed/stripped signal on a protected-path diff does **not flip the derived class**." DOD-11 requires the forge/replay/strip corpus red in both directions. This is satisfiable by producer-out-of-drive-reach + in-tree replay-binding + fail-closed, without cryptographic unforgeability-by-anyone. — source: docs/prds/argus-boundary-enforcement/effective.md FR-12, DOD-11.
- **F-7**: Incident I1 — a class's own first gate cannot gate its introducing PR; operator review is the sole backstop for that one bootstrap PR. The produced-signal transition has the exact analog (the signal begins existing at a point in history). — source: docs/observability/incidents.md I1; the frozen design's §3.9/R5 and the basis DOD-7/UB-5.
- **F-8**: The gate evaluates the PR's own commit range (`base..HEAD`, the resolved merge base to HEAD) — the shipped `Evaluate` behavior — so pre-mechanism history is never in a later PR's evaluated range and cannot be retroactively refused. — source: internal/argus/attribution/derive.go `Evaluate`.
- **F-9**: The design prose must satisfy the `check-content-paths` purity gate — content cites templates by version pin and skills/guides by role, never a system-footprint literal (a `.pyxis/`/`.claude/` path in a newly-added design markdown is a purity defect). — source: internal/docgate/gate/reg_check_content_paths.go (the parent's F-8).
